How to stop websites redirecting you in Microsoft Edge

A redirect is not one single browser problem. First identify whether the current tab changes address, a second tab opens, or pages appear without any click. That distinction tells you which protection can help.

Short answerKeep Edge's pop-up blocker and SmartScreen enabled, remove unfamiliar notification permissions and extensions, and scan the device if pages open by themselves. If one website attaches an unrelated tab to an ordinary click, compare the destination you selected with the destination that actually opened.

1. Check what your click actually selected

Before clicking, hover over a visible link and look at the destination shown by Edge. If that same destination opens in the current tab, the browser followed the declared link. If a different tab opens at the same moment, the site may have attached a popup or click layer to the action.

  • The declared destination opens: decide whether you trust the link before selecting it.
  • An unrelated second tab opens: treat it as a popup or click-hijack pattern.
  • The current tab visits several domains: it may be a server-side redirect chain; inspect the final site and leave if it is unexpected.
  • Pages appear without a click: review notifications, extensions, and installed software rather than relying on a page-level blocker.

2. Keep Edge's built-in pop-up blocking on

  1. Open Edge Settings.
  2. Choose Cookies and site permissions.
  3. Open Pop-ups and redirects.
  4. Keep Block enabled and remove unfamiliar sites from the allowed list.

Microsoft explains that the built-in setting blocks pop-up windows, but it does not remove every kind of advertisement or page redirect. Follow the official Edge pop-up troubleshooting steps first.

3. Remove unwanted notification permissions

In Settings → Cookies and site permissions → Notifications, remove sites you do not recognize. A browser notification can look like an operating-system warning and open a page when selected. A site should not need notification permission merely to play a video or prove that you are human.

4. Review extensions and keep SmartScreen enabled

Open Extensions → Manage extensions and disable anything unfamiliar. Keep Microsoft Defender SmartScreen enabled under Settings → Privacy, search, and services → Security. SmartScreen checks suspicious pages and downloads against Microsoft's reputation systems; read Microsoft's explanation of how SmartScreen protects browsing.

Test the remaining click-hijack pattern safely

PopIntent is designed for a narrow case: you select one visible destination and the site opens an unrelated popup or captures the click with a high-confidence transparent overlay. It makes the decision locally and keeps a short domain-only history on the device.

It is not an antivirus product, a general ad blocker, or a promise to stop every redirect. Default mode deliberately permits ambiguous sign-in, payment, and document flows.

If tabs open even when you do nothing

Restart Edge with unfamiliar extensions disabled. If pages continue to appear across unrelated sites or while Edge is idle, scan the device with Windows Security or another trusted security tool. Do not enter passwords, payment details, or recovery codes into a page reached through an unexpected redirect.

Report a reproducible case

If the issue happens on a public page, record the visible control you selected, the destination it declared, and the unrelated destination that opened. You can send a structured report without including private URLs, browsing history, account pages, or personal screenshots.
